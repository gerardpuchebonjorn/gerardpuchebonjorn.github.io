notes on multivariable calculus

multivariable calculus felt very different to me from calculus I (I call it multivariable to avoid confusion, but at least in europe calc 2 is basically multivariable calc). it felt a lot more conceptual: I could use my imagination and intuition much more than in calculus I.

one instance of this is the visual approach to lagrange multipliers, where you can see how the constraint curve and the level curves meet, and therefore why the gradients need to be proportional at the optimum.

there are a ton of examples: the jacobian when you change coordinates, because the little squares change in size; thinking about how to parametrise spirals or cylinders; etc. it is all quite accessible to deduce yourself if you think about it carefully, and it is indeed beautiful.

going deeper

all of vector calculus is also super nice to learn: green's, stokes' and gauss's theorems. Steve Burton's videos on these are amazing and give you a sharp understanding.

it is also a great moment to learn gradient descent if you are interested (my university course covered it), as it is literally optimising a multivariable function. you'll see very clearly why the learning rate matters, why you can get stuck in local minima, and all the intuition behind it.

on the tools

other than this, it is important to get comfortable with other coordinate systems, especially polar and cylindrical, and to be flexible with changing the order of integration, changing differentials and parametrising. basically what you did in calculus I (get cracked at the tools), but now with more variables and new tools.
